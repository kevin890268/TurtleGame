ACTION_ID: 13

ACTION_NAME: flip

CONTEXT: ABOVE WATER --- the turtle lies UPSIDE DOWN: the CARAPACE rests
on an invisible flat ground, the plastron faces up, and all four legs
are in the air.

DESCRIPTION: The turtle is stuck UPSIDE DOWN on its carapace and tries
to turn itself back over.

CRITICAL PHYSICAL ROTATION RULE: FLIP is a REAL whole-body physical
rotation.

The turtle does NOT keep its head or face upright while the body is
upside down.

When the turtle flips onto its back:

-   The ENTIRE turtle rotates together.
-   The head and neck rotate together with the body.
-   The face orientation must also be upside down.
-   The eyes, mouth, nostrils and facial stripes must follow the same
    physical rotation.
-   Do NOT rotate or redraw the head independently to make the face look
    upright.
-   Do NOT make the turtle's face appear normally upright while the
    plastron is facing upward.
-   The head must remain anatomically attached to the correct end of the
    body and shell.
-   The neck must emerge naturally from the front/neck opening of the
    upside-down body.
-   The facial markings must rotate consistently with the head.
-   The shell, plastron, head, neck, legs and tail are one physically
    connected body.

IMPORTANT VISUAL TEST: Compare the FLIP turtle with the normal turtle
reference.

If the normal turtle is rotated 180 degrees as one complete object, the
FLIP turtle's head and face must have the same resulting orientation.

The face is NOT a separate upright element.

Do NOT "correct" the face orientation for readability.

================================================== 1. FRAME SEQUENCE
==================================================

FRAME 1: On its back, plastron facing up, all four legs waving in the
air. The head and face are already physically upside down together with
the body.

FRAME 2: Head and neck stretch out and push against the invisible
ground; the whole shell rocks to one side. The head remains physically
rotated with the upside-down body. Do not straighten the face.

FRAME 3: Strongest rock: the entire shell tilts far to one side, with
legs reaching. The head and face follow the same body rotation and
remain anatomically upside down.

FRAME 4: Shell rocks back toward the centre; legs wave again. The head
remains attached and physically inverted with the body. Frame 4 must
lead naturally back into Frame 1.

LOOP: Frame 4 must transition naturally back into Frame 1.

The four frames must clearly belong to the SAME continuous movement.

IMPORTANT: Follow rules section 29 (FLIP). Calm expression, no injury,
no blood, no distress marks.

================================================== 5. FACE ORIENTATION
--- CRITICAL ==================================================

This section overrides any tendency to make the character face look
upright or cute.

For FLIP, the turtle is physically upside down.

Therefore:

NORMAL TURTLE: head and face have normal anatomical orientation.

FLIPPED TURTLE: the whole head has rotated together with the body.

Therefore the face is also upside down.

DO NOT: - keep eyes in a visually upright arrangement - rotate only the
head back upright - rotate only the face - redraw the mouth to appear
upright - make the facial stripes ignore the rotation - make the head
look like it has been attached separately - use a "camera-facing normal
face" on an upside-down turtle

DO: - rotate the complete head and neck with the body - preserve the
exact same head proportions - preserve the same eye size and placement
relative to the head - preserve the same striped pattern - preserve the
same mouth and snout anatomy - preserve the same connection between
head, neck and shell

The viewer should immediately understand: "This is the same turtle,
lying on its back."

Not: "This is a turtle body with a normally upright head attached."
