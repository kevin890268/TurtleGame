# Turtle Sprite Generation Rules
# Chinese Three-Striped Box Turtle / 斑龜
# Version 3.1

==================================================
1. CHARACTER REFERENCE
==================================================

The uploaded image:

character_reference.png

is the PRIMARY and AUTHORITATIVE visual reference
for the turtle character.

The turtle shown in the reference image is ONE EXACT
individual turtle.

Every generated sprite must depict this SAME individual.

Do not replace the turtle with a generic turtle.

Do not redesign the turtle.

The reference image defines the turtle's physical identity.

==================================================
2. SPECIES
==================================================

Species:

Chinese three-striped box turtle
Chinese three-striped pond turtle
Chinese stripe-necked turtle

Chinese name:

斑龜

Scientific name:

Mauremys reevesii

The turtle must unmistakably look like a
Chinese three-striped box turtle.

Do NOT substitute another turtle species.

NOT:

- red-eared slider
- yellow-bellied slider
- painted turtle
- map turtle
- musk turtle
- snapping turtle
- tortoise
- softshell turtle
- sea turtle
- generic green turtle

==================================================
3. CHARACTER IDENTITY
==================================================

The following physical characteristics are LOCKED.

They must remain consistent across:

- every action
- every frame
- every view
- every sprite sheet

Only these may change:

- animation pose
- body orientation
- viewing direction
- frame of motion

The following must NOT change.

--------------------------------------------------
SHELL
--------------------------------------------------

The shell is the dominant visual mass.

Preserve:

- dark blackish-brown coloration
- deep olive-brown coloration
- elongated oval silhouette
- moderately domed shell
- substantial shell volume
- clear individual scutes
- dark scute boundaries
- subtle warm orange-brown / ochre markings
- natural irregular shell markings
- darker outer shell edge

The shell must remain DARK.

Do NOT make the shell:

- bright green
- bright yellow
- bright orange
- colorful
- transparent
- plastic-looking

--------------------------------------------------
THREE SHELL KEELS
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle has three clearly visible longitudinal
shell ridges / keels.

They are physical structures of the shell.

There must be:

- one central keel
- one left keel
- one right keel

The three keels follow the natural curvature
of the shell.

They must remain visible whenever the viewing angle
allows them to be seen.

Do NOT turn the keels into painted decorative stripes.

Do NOT flatten the shell.

--------------------------------------------------
SHELL MARKINGS
--------------------------------------------------

Preserve the natural dark shell markings from
the reference character.

Warm orange-brown / ochre markings may appear
inside individual scutes.

These markings must remain:

- subtle
- natural
- irregular
- integrated into the shell

Do NOT turn them into:

- large orange spots
- leopard spots
- bright yellow stripes
- decorative symbols

--------------------------------------------------
HEAD
--------------------------------------------------

The head is relatively small compared with the shell.

Preserve:

- narrow elongated turtle head
- natural rounded snout
- yellow-green / olive-green base color
- small dark eye
- natural turtle eye proportions

Do NOT create:

- oversized head
- anime eyes
- mammal-like face
- cartoon frog face

--------------------------------------------------
FACE AND NECK STRIPES
--------------------------------------------------

The head and neck contain dense dark longitudinal
striping.

This is one of the most important identifying
features of this turtle.

Preserve:

- yellow-green base skin
- numerous narrow dark stripes
- longitudinal direction
- organic irregularity
- continuation of stripes from head to neck

Do NOT simplify the markings into only
two or three large stripes.

Do NOT replace them with spots.

Do NOT remove the stripes.

--------------------------------------------------
LEGS
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle has four short but sturdy legs.

The legs are dark olive-brown / blackish
with narrow yellow-green longitudinal stripes.

Preserve:

- natural turtle limb anatomy
- sturdy front legs
- larger rear legs
- clearly separated toes
- semi-aquatic turtle foot structure

Do NOT create:

- mammal paws
- frog feet
- plain green legs
- plain brown legs

--------------------------------------------------
TAIL
--------------------------------------------------

The tail is visible and tapered.

Preserve:

- natural turtle tail
- relatively long tapered shape
- narrow tip
- natural connection to rear shell

Do NOT create:

- lizard-like tail
- oversized tail
- invisible tiny tail

==================================================
4. BODY PROPORTIONS
==================================================

The turtle should remain:

- cute
- believable
- anatomically coherent
- naturally proportioned

The shell is much larger than the head.

The head must NOT become oversized.

The eyes must NOT become oversized.

The body must NOT become chibi.

Do not use exaggerated cartoon proportions.

The turtle should feel like a real young turtle
translated into a children's picture-book character.

==================================================
5. CAMERA / 2.5D VIEW
==================================================

The game uses a 2.5D presentation.

Preferred camera angle:

35–45 degree 3/4 perspective.

The turtle should NOT normally be shown as a
perfect flat side profile.

The turtle should have visible three-dimensional volume.

The preferred 3/4 view should allow the viewer
to see several of the following:

- face
- one side of the head
- front of shell
- side of shell
- shell depth
- shell keels
- near-side legs
- portions of far-side legs
- tail

The turtle must feel like a 2D illustration
representing a consistent 3D object.

==================================================
6. FOUR CORE VIEWS
==================================================

The sprite system supports four primary views:

F = Front
R = Right
B = Back
L = Left

These are TRUE physical views of the SAME turtle.

--------------------------------------------------
F — FRONT
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle faces the viewer.

Show:

- face
- both eyes
- facial stripes
- front shell
- front legs
- appropriate portions of rear legs

The turtle must retain natural 3/4 depth
where appropriate.

--------------------------------------------------
R — RIGHT
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle faces screen-right.

Show:

- right-side shell volume
- head and neck
- front/rear leg relationship
- tail
- shell thickness

Do not make this a perfectly flat orthographic side view.

--------------------------------------------------
B — BACK
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle faces away from the viewer.

Show:

- rear shell
- shell scutes
- three-dimensional shell volume
- rear legs
- tail

The head may be partially hidden by the shell.

B must be a TRUE rear view.

Do NOT horizontally flip the Front view.

--------------------------------------------------
L — LEFT
--------------------------------------------------

The turtle faces screen-left.

Show:

- left-side shell volume
- head and neck
- front/rear leg relationship
- tail
- shell thickness

L must be a TRUE left-side view.

Do NOT simply mirror the R image as the final artwork.

==================================================
7. VIEW CONSISTENCY
==================================================

The four views must look like the SAME physical turtle.

Preserve across F/R/B/L:

- shell width
- shell height
- shell silhouette
- shell markings
- three shell keels
- head size
- head shape
- neck length
- eye size
- facial stripes
- body length
- leg length
- toe structure
- tail shape
- overall proportions

Only the viewing direction changes.

The turtle must NOT become a different character
when changing views.

==================================================
8. ACTION CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Every action uses the SAME character.

Every action must preserve:

- shell
- shell color
- shell markings
- shell keels
- head
- facial stripes
- neck
- eyes
- legs
- toes
- tail
- body proportions
- art style

The action changes the POSE.

The action does NOT redesign the character.

==================================================
9. FRAME CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Every action contains exactly:

4 frames.

Frame structure:

Frame 1:
Beginning of movement.

Frame 2:
Movement developing.

Frame 3:
Strongest / most extended part
of the movement.

Frame 4:
Returning toward the starting state.

For looping actions:

Frame 4 must transition naturally
back to Frame 1.

Use:

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 1

Do NOT create four unrelated poses.

The four frames must clearly belong
to the SAME continuous movement.

==================================================
10. ANATOMY RULES
==================================================

Maintain believable turtle anatomy.

Do NOT:

- add limbs
- remove limbs
- add toes
- remove toes
- create impossible joints
- bend limbs unnaturally
- detach body parts
- stretch the shell
- squash the shell
- deform the head
- allow limbs to pass through the shell
- create duplicated limbs
- create duplicated heads
- create duplicated tails

The shell remains physically rigid.

The body, neck, legs and tail provide most
of the visible animation.

==================================================

==================================================

Motion should be:

- readable

- suitable for a cozy turtle-raising game

Avoid extreme cartoon deformation.

Do NOT use:

- squash and stretch
- smear frames
- motion blur
- speed lines
- exaggerated deformation
- impossible poses

The movement should come from believable
turtle anatomy.

==================================================
14. SPRITE SHEET FORMAT
==================================================

Each generated image contains:

4 × 4 = 16 cells.

The 4 columns are animation frames:

Column 1 = Frame 1
Column 2 = Frame 2
Column 3 = Frame 3
Column 4 = Frame 4

The 4 rows are views:

Row 1 = F
Row 2 = R
Row 3 = B
Row 4 = L

Layout:

┌────────┬────────┬────────┬────────┐
│ F-1    │ F-2    │ F-3    │ F-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ R-1    │ R-2    │ R-3    │ R-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ B-1    │ B-2    │ B-3    │ B-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ L-1    │ L-2    │ L-3    │ L-4    │
└────────┴────────┴────────┴────────┘

Exactly 16 turtle cells.

No additional characters.

No extra poses outside the grid.

==================================================
15. CELL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Every cell must use:

- same character scale
- same camera distance
- same visual size
- same art style
- same lighting
- same rendering quality

Keep the shell center approximately aligned.

Keep the turtle inside the cell.

Do NOT crop:

- head
- shell
- legs
- tail

Do NOT randomly move the turtle
between cells.

==================================================
16. BACKGROUND
==================================================

The background MUST be:

PURE SOLID MAGENTA

RGB:

255, 0, 255

The background must be completely flat.

No:

- gradient
- texture
- shadow
- floor
- water
- environment
- scenery

The magenta background exists specifically
for automatic sprite extraction.

==================================================
18. SPECIAL RULE — EAT
==================================================

The `eat` animation shows the turtle eating.

However:

NO FOOD OBJECT should appear in the sprite sheet.

Show only the turtle's eating movement:

- lower head
- approach eating position
- bite motion
- return upward

The game engine handles the actual food object
and gameplay logic separately.

==================================================
19. SPECIAL RULE — POOP
==================================================

The `poop` animation represents the turtle
performing an elimination action.

The sprite must contain ONLY the turtle.

Do NOT draw:

- poop
- feces
- fecal particles
- poop pile
- floating waste
- detached objects

Suggested motion:

Frame 1:
Turtle settles down.

Frame 2:
Rear body rises slightly and tail shifts naturally.

Frame 3:
Turtle holds the elimination posture.

Frame 4:
Turtle returns to normal posture.

Game logic handles:

water_dirty += POOP_DIRTINESS

The animation itself does NOT contain a poop object.

==================================================
20. SPECIAL RULE — WATER ACTIONS
==================================================

The turtle is semi-aquatic.

Water actions are part of the standard
animation system.

These include:

enter_water
swim
swim_fast
swim_turn
dive
rise
float
surface

However, the sprite sheet itself still uses
the same solid magenta background.

Do NOT draw:

- water
- bubbles
- splash effects
- waves
- fish
- plants
- underwater scenery

The animation must communicate swimming
through the turtle's body and limb movement alone.

The turtle must NOT develop fish-like anatomy.

==================================================
21. TURN ACTION
==================================================

`turn` is a REAL body rotation.

Do NOT simply rotate the head.

Do NOT slide the turtle sideways.

Do NOT create a static alternate pose.

The shell and body must rotate together.

Example:

F → R

Frame 1:
F

Frame 2:
between F and R

Frame 3:
close to R

Frame 4:
R

Likewise:

F → L
R → B
B → L
L → F

The body, shell, head and legs must
participate naturally in the turn.

==================================================
26. MOST IMPORTANT RULE
==================================================

The priority order is:

1. SAME INDIVIDUAL TURTLE
2. CORRECT SPECIES
3. CORRECT CHARACTER FEATURES
4. CONSISTENT 2.5D VIEWS
5. CORRECT ACTION
6. CORRECT FRAME MOTION
7. CONSISTENT SPRITE SCALE
8. JAPANESE PICTURE-BOOK STYLE

When style and character identity conflict:

CHARACTER IDENTITY WINS.

When animation and anatomy conflict:

ANATOMICAL CONSISTENCY WINS.

When visual effects and sprite extraction conflict:

CLEAN SPRITE EXTRACTION WINS.

The goal is NOT to create 32 different turtle drawings.

The goal is to create ONE exact turtle character
that can perform 32 different actions,
with 4 frames and 4 views for every action.
