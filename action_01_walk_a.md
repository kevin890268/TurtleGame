# ACTION 01 — WALK A
# 悠閒散步 / Relaxed Walking

ACTION_ID:
01

ACTION_NAME:
walk_a

DESCRIPTION:
悠閒、自然、輕鬆地向前行走。

The turtle performs a calm, relaxed and natural
forward walking cycle.

This is a young turtle casually walking around
its environment.

The movement should feel slow, gentle and peaceful.

This is NOT running.

This is NOT a frightened movement.

This is NOT an aggressive movement.

This is NOT a mammal-style walking or running motion.

==================================================
1. ANIMATION GOAL
==================================================

Create ONE continuous four-frame walking cycle.

The four frames must clearly represent
different phases of the SAME walking movement.

Frame sequence:

F1 → F2 → F3 → F4 → F1

The final frame must transition naturally
back into Frame 1.

The movement should be subtle enough that
the turtle still feels like a real turtle.

==================================================
2. FRAME 1 — START
==================================================

The turtle begins in a relaxed walking posture.

The body is low and stable.

The shell remains level and rigid.

The head is slightly extended forward.

The neck is relaxed.

The turtle is beginning a step.

One front leg is slightly forward.

The opposite rear leg is positioned naturally
to support the body.

The remaining legs maintain stable contact
with the ground.

This frame should feel like the beginning
of a slow walking cycle.

Do not make the turtle look frozen.

Do not make the pose exaggerated.

==================================================
3. FRAME 2 — FIRST STEP
==================================================

The turtle moves slightly forward.

One front leg advances naturally.

The diagonally opposite rear leg also changes
position to support the forward movement.

The opposite pair of legs remains relatively stable.

The body shifts slightly forward.

The shell remains rigid.

The head remains relatively stable.

The neck does not dramatically stretch.

The movement should be small and believable.

The turtle should still look relaxed.

==================================================
4. FRAME 3 — OPPOSITE STEP
==================================================

The opposite pair of legs now takes the next step.

The previously advancing legs move toward
their supporting position.

The turtle has moved slightly farther forward.

The body shifts naturally.

The shell remains level and rigid.

The head remains calm and stable.

The neck remains naturally extended.

This is the strongest walking phase
of the four-frame cycle.

The leg positions must be visibly different
from Frame 2.

==================================================
5. FRAME 4 — RETURN
==================================================

The turtle completes the walking cycle.

The legs transition naturally toward
the starting configuration.

The body continues moving slightly forward.

The shell remains rigid.

The head remains stable.

The turtle remains relaxed.

The final pose must connect naturally
back to Frame 1.

Frame 4 must NOT look like a completely
different pose.

==================================================
6. WALKING MECHANICS
==================================================

Use natural turtle locomotion.

The turtle walks slowly.

The legs perform the primary movement.

The body moves only slightly.

The shell does not bounce dramatically.

The shell does not squash or stretch.

The head and neck remain relatively stable.

The turtle should not move like a dog,
cat, horse or other mammal.

Do NOT use exaggerated leg motion.

Do NOT make the turtle stand upright.

Do NOT make the belly drag unnaturally.

Do NOT make the turtle slide without
corresponding leg movement.

==================================================
7. LEG MOVEMENT
==================================================

Maintain four anatomically connected legs.

The walking cycle should use natural
alternating limb movement.

The legs must remain attached to the body.

The feet remain in contact with the ground
when appropriate.

Toes remain anatomically believable.

Do not create:

- extra legs
- missing legs
- duplicated legs
- twisted legs
- detached feet
- impossible joints

==================================================
8. HEAD AND NECK
==================================================

The head remains calm during walking.

The neck may move very slightly
with the walking motion.

Do NOT dramatically bob the head.

Do NOT extend the neck excessively.

Do NOT retract the head into the shell.

The head and neck should remain consistent
with the character_reference.png.

==================================================
9. SHELL
==================================================

The shell remains structurally rigid
throughout all four frames.

Do NOT:

- squash the shell
- stretch the shell
- bend the shell
- rotate the shell independently
- change shell markings
- change shell proportions

The three longitudinal shell keels remain
consistent across all frames.

Shell markings remain unchanged.

==================================================
10. MOVEMENT DIRECTION
==================================================

The turtle moves naturally forward
relative to its current orientation.

For each view:

F:
The turtle walks toward the forward direction
of the character.

R:
The turtle walks toward screen-right /
character-forward direction.

B:
The turtle walks toward the character's
forward direction, away from the viewer.

L:
The turtle walks toward screen-left /
character-forward direction.

The action represents the SAME physical walking
movement viewed from four directions.

==================================================
11. FOUR VIEW CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Generate the same walking cycle in all four views.

Row 1:
F — Front 3/4

Row 2:
R — Right 3/4

Row 3:
B — Back 3/4

Row 4:
L — Left 3/4

The character must remain the SAME individual
in all four rows.

Do not redesign the turtle for different views.

Do not change:

- shell shape
- shell markings
- shell keels
- head
- facial stripes
- eyes
- legs
- toes
- tail
- proportions

Only the viewing direction changes.

==================================================
12. SPRITE SHEET LAYOUT
==================================================

Create exactly ONE 4 × 4 sprite sheet.

4 columns:

Column 1 = Frame 1
Column 2 = Frame 2
Column 3 = Frame 3
Column 4 = Frame 4

4 rows:

Row 1 = F
Row 2 = R
Row 3 = B
Row 4 = L

Layout:

┌────────┬────────┬────────┬────────┐
│ F-1    │ F-2    │ F-3    │ F-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ R-1    │ R-2    │ R-3    │ R-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ B-1    │ B-2    │ B-3    │ B-4    │
├────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
│ L-1    │ L-2    │ L-3    │ L-4    │
└────────┴────────┴────────┴────────┘

Exactly 16 cells.

Exactly ONE turtle per cell.

No additional characters.

==================================================
13. CELL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Keep the turtle approximately the same:

- size
- scale
- position
- shell center
- visual weight

across all 16 cells.

The entire turtle must remain inside
its cell.

Do not crop:

- head
- shell
- legs
- tail

Do not allow neighboring cells to overlap.

==================================================
14. BACKGROUND
==================================================

Use a completely uniform solid
chroma-key magenta background.

Exact RGB:

255, 0, 255

No:

- floor
- grass
- water
- environment
- rocks
- plants
- shadows
- gradients
- textures
- particles
- effects

Only the turtle appears in each cell.

==================================================
15. NO EXTRA OBJECTS
==================================================

Do NOT include:

- food
- toys
- rocks
- plants
- water
- dirt
- dust
- footprints
- particles
- motion lines
- speed lines
- text
- numbers
- labels
- UI
- watermark

==================================================
16. STYLE
==================================================

Follow the art style defined in rules.md.

The walking animation should remain:

- cute
- warm
- peaceful
- natural
- hand-drawn
- suitable for a Japanese children's
  picture-book turtle game

Do not exaggerate the movement for comedy.

==================================================
17. FINAL ANIMATION REQUIREMENT
==================================================

The final 4 × 4 sheet must communicate:

ONE EXACT SAME young Chinese three-striped
box turtle performing ONE continuous,
slow and relaxed walking cycle.

The four frames must be clearly different
but smoothly connected.

The four views must represent the same
physical turtle from different directions.

The animation must loop naturally:

F1 → F2 → F3 → F4 → F1

Prioritize:

1. character consistency
2. anatomical correctness
3. clear walking motion
4. view consistency
5. smooth frame transition
6. Japanese picture-book style

END OF ACTION 01
